Most talk about waste-based oils is about sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) and renewable diesel. A quieter market sits below them: fuels made from fatty-acid residues, burned in equipment that runs on heavy fuel oil today. In South Korea this is called **bio heavy oil**. This post covers what that market is, why buyers use it, what they need to plan for, and where SafOil's **SJ50 mixed fatty acid** fits.

## What bio heavy oil is

Bio heavy oil is a fuel blended from animal fat, vegetable oil, fatty acid methyl or ethyl esters (FAME/FAEE) and their by-products. Compared with fossil heavy oil (bunker C), it has **lower viscosity and a higher acid value**, because it contains less paraffin and aromatics ([Journal of the Korean Applied Science and Technology](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201810263415754.page)).

It uses lower-grade fatty materials that SAF and HVO plants find hard to process. So it does not compete head-on with them for premium used cooking oil.

## Why buyers use it

### 1. Power generation: a market built on policy

South Korea is the clearest example. The government tested bio heavy oil at **five bunker-C power stations from 2014**. It found the fuel a workable substitute and approved it for commercial power generation from **January 2019** ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/1777640); [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/south-korea-adding-bio-heavy-oil-as-renewable-energy-resource/); [Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/south-korea-approves-use-bio-heavy-oil-power-generation.html)).

Korea counts bio heavy oil as a renewable energy source ([POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/south-korea-adding-bio-heavy-oil-as-renewable-energy-resource/)). Large generators must supply part of their power from renewables under the Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS) ([Chambers Renewable Energy 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/renewable-energy-2026/south-korea)). Supply contracts keep being awarded. KG Eco Solution won a 2025 contract from Korea Southern Power, and Asia Energy secured contracts with two Korean generators in December 2025 ([Quantum, Dec 2024](https://www.qcintel.com/biofuels/article/south-korean-biofuels-firm-wins-supply-contract-for-bio-heavy-oil-report-33916.html); [Quantum, Dec 2025](https://www.qcintel.com/biofuels/article/asia-energy-to-supply-biofuel-to-south-korean-power-generation-firms-report-55334.html)).

**What this means for blenders:** the end buyers are regulated utilities that buy through tenders. Blenders who supply them need feedstock with a steady specification and paperwork for every shipment.

### 2. Industrial boilers and furnaces: lower emissions without new equipment

The emission figures explain why industry is interested. Compared with heavy oil, bio heavy oil produces **almost no sulphur oxides, 39% less nitrogen oxide, 28% less fine dust and 85% less greenhouse gas**, according to the Korea Petroleum Quality and Distribution Authority ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/1777640)).

It can also be used in **existing bunker-C equipment**, so the upfront cost is lower than for most other renewable options ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/1777640)). Heavy industry is now looking at it too. In June 2026, Korea's glass manufacturers agreed with the Korean Bioenergy Association to explore bio heavy oil for their operations ([Quantum](https://www.qcintel.com/biofuels/article/s-korea-glass-industry-bioenergy-group-to-explore-bio-heavy-oil-use-reports-67359.html)).

**What this means for plant operators:** the fuel can be switched without rebuilding the plant. The work goes into matching burner settings, preheating and storage to a fatty-acid fuel.

### 3. Marine fuel: the fastest-growing outlet

Singapore, the world's largest bunkering hub, sold a record **1.36 million tonnes of biofuel blends in 2025, up 54.4% on 2024**. Sales of bio-blended high-sulphur fuel oil (HSFO) **tripled** ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/011426-singapores-biobunker-sales-hit-record-high-of-136-mil-mt-in-2025)). Ship operators are buying heavy-fuel blends, not just distillates.

Sea trials back this up. A study of a bio-marine fuel blend found **no change in engine performance** and **16% lower well-to-wake CO2** than the reference fuel ([Renewable Energy, 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v219y2023ip2s0960148123014581.html)). The revised marine fuel standard, ISO 8217:2024, now includes bio-blended fuels ([UK P&I Club](https://www.ukpandi.com/news-and-resources/articles/2024/iso-8217-2024-the-marine-fuel-standard-has-been-updated)).

**What this means for bunker suppliers and blenders:** demand exists, but the blend has to meet the marine standard and the engine's limits. That is set by blending and specification, not by the feedstock alone.

### 4. Beyond fuel: oleochemicals, feed and lubricants

Mixed fatty acids are also an industrial raw material. Depending on the grade, they are used to make soaps and surfactants, calcium salts of fatty acids for animal feed, and metallic soaps for lubricants and greases.

## What to plan for before switching

The same trials that show the benefits also show what needs care:

- **NOx can rise at first.** In the marine trial, NOx rose 17% until injection timing was adjusted to meet IMO limits; the adjustment then cut NOx by 13% ([Renewable Energy, 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v219y2023ip2s0960148123014581.html)).
- **Keep the fuel warm.** Fuel transferred below **50°C** clogged the vessel's particle filter ([Renewable Energy, 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v219y2023ip2s0960148123014581.html)).
- **Acid value is higher than fossil heavy oil** ([Journal of the Korean Applied Science and Technology](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201810263415754.page)). Check storage materials, blend ratio and the limits in your local fuel standard.

The common thread is simple: **agree the specification, heating and blending plan before the first shipment.**
