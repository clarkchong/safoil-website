Picture the end of a refining run at a vegetable oil plant. The oil that people will cook with has gone downstream. What is left behind is dark, thick and acidic, and for a long time it was sold for soap or animal feed, or simply written off. Today the same material is quoted by the tonne in European and Asian biofuel markets.

The trouble is that buyers keep using one word, "acid oil", for products that behave quite differently. Mixing them up is an expensive mistake, so this post separates them.

## Two by-products, two refining routes

**Soapstock acid oil** comes from refining oils such as soybean, sunflower or rapeseed. When the refiner removes free fatty acids with an alkali, they leave as soapstock. Treat that soapstock with sulfuric or hydrochloric acid and the soaps turn back into free fatty acids. The result is acid oil: a dark product, typically 50 to 90 percent free fatty acid, also sold as acidulated soapstock. (We cover it in more depth in our guides to [acidulated soapstock](/blog/acidulated-soapstock-feedstock/) and [vegetable acid oils](/blog/vegetable-acid-oils-explained/).)

**PFAD**, short for palm fatty acid distillate, comes from refining crude palm oil. Physical refining strips the free fatty acids out by distillation instead of by alkali, and what is collected is PFAD. Published patent literature describes a free fatty acid content of at least 60 percent, and a laboratory analysis of one sample measured close to 93 percent.

In short, the two are made by different methods, from different crops, in different places.

## Why free fatty acid content matters so much

Free fatty acid (FFA) is the number that decides what a plant can do with a feedstock.

Conventional biodiesel is made by transesterification, and feedstocks with FFA above about 3 percent cause reaction problems and poor conversion unless they are first treated. Acid oils sit far above that line, so a biodiesel plant has to add an esterification step or blend them in small proportions with cleaner oil.

HVO and SAF plants face a different challenge. Hydrotreating is less bothered by fatty acids themselves, but its quality requirements for contaminants are stricter than for biodiesel. Phosphorus and metals poison the catalyst. Pretreatment specialists note that low-quality material, including some acid oils, can need extra heat treatment to bring those contaminants low enough. For the poorest grades, an alternative process of fat splitting and fatty acid distillation exists. It handles very dirty feedstock but usually gives lower yields and carries much higher capital and operating cost.

The practical consequence is that two cargoes labelled "acid oil" can have very different value to the same plant. A buyer's real question is not what it is called but how clean it is.

## What a spec sheet should tell you

A useful specification goes beyond the headline FFA figure. As an illustration of the level of detail serious buyers expect, one published price benchmark for Indonesian PFAD specifies at least 70 percent free fatty acid, at least 95 percent saponifiable fatty matter and a maximum of 1 percent moisture and impurities. Whatever the grade, ask for:

- **Free fatty acid and acid value.** The basic measure of how much of the oil is fatty acid.
- **Moisture and impurities.** Water and solids add cost and can foul equipment.
- **Phosphorus and metals.** The figures HVO and SAF producers watch most closely.
- **Unsaponifiable matter.** Material that does not convert to fuel and is lost in yield.
- **Origin and refining route.** Soapstock versus distillate, and which crop.

Laboratory data from an independent inspector, not just the seller's own certificate, is worth requesting on a first order.

## The market behind the product

Both products are priced against competing uses and against the wider vegetable oil complex, which helps explain why they move.

Fastmarkets began assessing soapstock acid oil delivered to Amsterdam, Rotterdam and Antwerp on 8 May 2025. Between May and December, the price swung by $150 per tonne, or 17.9 percent, amid volatility at origin in South America and the Middle East and North Africa.

PFAD tracks palm oil. Industry sources told Platts that PFAD and palm stearin prices are expected to follow crude palm oil, and Indonesia's biodiesel programme matters because it pulls palm volumes away from export. One secondary price source puts PFAD FOB Malaysia at roughly $985 to $1,000 per tonne in early January 2026, climbing to nearly $1,150 by mid-March as crude oil prices rose. Treat that as a guide to direction, not a quote. (For the longer view, see our [PFAD outlook](/blog/pfad-outlook/).)

## Which one suits which buyer

There is no universal winner. A rough guide:

- **An existing biodiesel plant** may value acid oils for lowering feedstock cost, provided it can add pretreatment.
- **An HVO or SAF producer** will care most about contaminants and whether its pretreatment unit can cope.
- **A trader** should care about classification and documentation, because the same cargo can be treated differently in different markets. Our [PFAD outlook](/blog/pfad-outlook/) touches on how PFAD's classification differs from waste streams such as UCO.
- **A buyer serving soap or oleochemical customers** is competing with fuel demand for the same tonnes.
