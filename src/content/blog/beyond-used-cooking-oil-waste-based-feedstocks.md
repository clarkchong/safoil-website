Used cooking oil (UCO) made up about 80% of the sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) supplied in the EU last year. Getting hold of it is getting harder. The next phase of the waste-based fuel market will be won by buyers who build a wider set of feedstocks now, before mandates in Asia take effect.

## Why UCO alone can no longer carry the market

Demand for UCO is growing far faster than the supply of oil that can be collected. Four forces are tightening the market at the same time:

- **Aviation has joined the queue.** EU airports received 1.1 million tonnes of SAF in 2025, almost six times the 193,000 tonnes of 2024, and the EU mandate rises from 2% to 6% by 2030 ([UkrAgroConsult / EASA](https://ukragroconsult.com/en/news/eu-saf-use-rises-nearly-sixfold-boosting-demand-for-used-oils/)). Analysts expect airlines to triple UCO demand by 2030 ([T&E](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/european-and-us-used-cooking-oil-demand-increasingly-unsustainable-analysis)).
- **The largest exporter is keeping more at home.** China's own SAF plants already use an estimated 100,000–120,000 tonnes of UCO a month. Monthly exports were projected to fall 20–40% below their 2024 average ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/energy-transition/022425-interview-policy-confusion-saf-demand-reshape-asian-used-cooking-oil-trade-prices)).
- **Indonesia has closed its doors.** Exports of UCO and palm oil mill effluent (POME) oil have been restricted since January 2025 to supply domestic biodiesel. The national B50 mandate began on 9 July 2026 ([UkrAgroConsult](https://ukragroconsult.com/en/news/indonesia-maintains-ban-on-crude-palm-oil-waste-and-used-cooking-oil-exports/); [Indonesia Investments](https://www.indonesia-investments.com/news/todays-headlines/indonesia-s-b50-mandate-driving-energy-independence-tightening-cpo-markets/item9976)).
- **Asia's own mandates are starting.** Singapore requires 1% SAF on departing flights from 2026. South Korea follows with 1% from 2027, Malaysia plans 1% from 2027, and Japan targets 10% by 2030 ([Sustainable Aviation Futures](https://www.sustainableaviationfutures.com/saf-spotlight/apac-saf-landscape)).

The ceiling is real. Global UCO potential is estimated at about 10 million tonnes a year ([Oliver Wyman](https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2024/mar/alternative-saf-technologies-for-feedstock-limitations.html)). Global SAF targets for 2030 would need at least twice the UCO that the US, Europe and China can collect combined ([T&E](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/european-and-us-used-cooking-oil-demand-increasingly-unsustainable-analysis)). For more on the demand side, see [why UCO demand is a structural story](/blog/uco-demand-structural-story/).

## Eight alternatives worth knowing

The strongest substitutes are residues from industries that already run at scale: palm milling and refining, vegetable oil processing, and meat rendering. Most work on the same HEFA/HVO pretreatment and hydroprocessing route as UCO ([Biobased Diesel](https://www.biobased-diesel.com/post/requirements-and-solutions-for-pretreatment-of-hvo-feedstocks)).

### 1. POME oil (palm oil mill effluent oil)

POME oil is the oily sludge recovered from palm mill wastewater. It equals only about 1.5% of crude palm oil output ([ScienceDirect review, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032126003059)). It is listed in Annex IX Part A of the EU Renewable Energy Directive (RED), the advanced feedstock list ([Sustainable Commodities](https://www.sustainablecommodities.eu/markets/annex-ix-feedstocks)). Malaysia became the main exporter after Indonesia's ban, and Malaysian spot prices reached US$1,048/t FOB in December 2025 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/121025-commodities-2026-regulatory-uncertainty-impedes-palm-oil-mill-effluent-trade)). The open question is scrutiny. Declared EU volumes once exceeded what the world could produce, and Germany and the Netherlands have considered cutting its credit from double to single counting. Our [advanced feedstock outlook](/blog/advanced-feedstock-outlook/) covers the Annex IX premium in more detail.

### 2. PFAD (palm fatty acid distillate)

PFAD is a by-product of physical refining of palm oil. It contains roughly 80–90% free fatty acids and makes up about 5% of palm oil output, or around 4 million tonnes a year in theory ([ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032126003059)). PFAD is a recognised by-product for CORSIA, aviation's global emissions scheme. Its EU and UK status differs by country, and some treat it as a co-product rather than a residue ([Rainforest Foundation Norway](https://dv719tqmsuwvb.cloudfront.net/documents/Publikasjoner/Andre-rapporter/RF_PFAD_report_1123_oppslag.pdf)). It is a good fit for HVO and biodiesel, but check each destination's rules before relying on it for SAF. See our [PFAD outlook](/blog/pfad-outlook/).

### 3. SBEO (spent bleaching earth oil)

Bleaching clay used in vegetable oil refining keeps 20–50% of its weight in trapped oil, which can be extracted ([SUAT Fuels](https://suatfuels.com/feedstocks/sbeo.html)). SBEO is listed in Annex IX Part A. Recovery plants already operate in Malaysia and Indonesia ([EcoOils](https://www.ecooilsenergy.com/)). Because Southeast Asia refines most of the world's palm oil, it is one of the region's least-used waste streams.

### 4. Tallow and animal fats

Tallow supplied 17% of SAF feedstock in 2025, second only to UCO. Q1 2026 prices were about US$1,373–1,405/t across the US, China and the Netherlands ([Fact.MR](https://www.factmr.com/report/tallow-market)). Australia produces about 673,000 tonnes a year and is a natural supplier to Asia-Pacific ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/world-tallow-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). The limit is that animals are not raised for their fat, so supply cannot grow to meet fuel demand. More in our [tallow feedstock guide](/blog/tallow-renewable-diesel-feedstock/).

### 5. Soapstock acid oil (SSAO)

SSAO comes from neutralising crude vegetable oils during refining and is rich in free fatty acids. Several EU countries already treat it as advanced, and Fastmarkets now runs a weekly price for it ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/soap-stock-acid-oil-price-launch-frequently-asked-questions/)). Prices moved US$150/t (17.9%) between May and December 2025, so expect volatility ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/european-biofuel-feedstock-prices-fluctuate-significantly-in-2025-market-expects-more-volatility-next-year-2026-preview/)). See [acidulated soapstock as a feedstock](/blog/acidulated-soapstock-feedstock/).

### 6. Brown grease and trap grease

This is fat captured in grease traps and wastewater systems. The US alone produces around 1.5 million tonnes a year ([Advanced BioFuels USA](https://advancedbiofuelsusa.info/how-new-subsidy-guidelines-could-make-brown-grease-the-new-gold)). It is covered by Annex IX of RED III, but its high impurity load needs fat splitting and distillation before hydroprocessing ([Biobased Diesel](https://www.biobased-diesel.com/post/requirements-and-solutions-for-pretreatment-of-hvo-feedstocks)).

### 7. Non-food oilseed crops

Carinata, camelina and pongamia are grown on degraded land or between food crops. Japan's Idemitsu started a pongamia trial plantation in Australia with Terviva in January 2026 ([Hydrocarbon Processing](https://hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2025/01/idemitsu-to-begin-trial-of-non-edible-oilseed-crop-for-saf/)). Large plantations are being developed elsewhere, such as Canopy Energies in Madagascar ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/012126-interview-canopy-plants-largest-pongamia-acreage-rooting-steady-saf-takeoff)). Carinata oil has been costed at about US$0.81/kg ([ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032126003059)). Volumes are still small, but this group can scale.

### 8. Microalgae oil

Algae can reach around 50% lipid content and needs no farmland. Reported costs of US$1.31–9.29/kg and difficult dewatering keep it at pilot scale for now ([ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032126003059)). It is a long-term option rather than a 2027 answer.

POME oil, PFAD, SBEO and tallow are the options that can be traded at volume today. The crops and algae are options for the 2030s.

## Where bio heavy oil fits

Bio heavy oil is a strong alternative outlet, but it isn't a like-for-like replacement for UCO in SAF. It is made from the lower-grade residues that SAF plants can't use well, and it burns in boilers, power plants and ship engines that run on heavy fuel oil today.

**What it is.** Bio heavy oil is blended from fatty-acid materials such as animal fats, residual cooking oil and biodiesel by-products. It has lower viscosity and a higher acid value than fossil heavy oil ([Korean Applied Science & Technology journal](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201810263415754.page)). Because it uses bottom-of-the-barrel residues, it doesn't compete head-on with HEFA plants for premium UCO.

**The evidence that it works:**

- **Proven in power generation.** South Korea tested bio heavy oil at five bunker-C power stations from 2014 and approved it for commercial power generation from 2019 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/1777640); [IEA Bioenergy](https://www.ieabioenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CountryReport2024_Korea_final.pdf)).
- **Clear emission gains.** Compared with heavy oil, it produces almost no sulphur oxides, 39% less nitrogen oxide, 28% less fine dust and 85% less greenhouse gas, according to the Korea Petroleum Quality and Distribution Authority ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/1777640)).
- **Low switching cost.** Existing bunker-C equipment can burn it, so buyers avoid new capital spending ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/1777640)).
- **Real, policy-backed demand.** Korean generators must source 15% of their power from renewables in 2026 under the Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS), and bio heavy oil is one route. Bio heavy oil tenders totalled about KRW 1.95 trillion between 2013 and 2025 ([Asia Business Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026073011144235911)). Korea's glass industry signed an agreement in June 2026 to explore it for furnaces ([Quantum](https://www.qcintel.com/biofuels/article/s-korea-glass-industry-bioenergy-group-to-explore-bio-heavy-oil-use-reports-67359.html)).
- **A growing marine market.** Singapore sold a record 1.36 million tonnes of biofuel bunker blends in 2025, up 54% on 2024 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/011426-singapores-biobunker-sales-hit-record-high-of-136-mil-mt-in-2025)). Ship trials found no loss of engine performance and 16% lower well-to-wake CO2 with a 20% bio heavy fuel blend ([Renewable Energy, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960148123014581)).

**What buyers should plan for.** The acid limit in the ISO 8217 marine fuel standard (2.5 mg KOH/g) caps marine blends at about 20%. NOx rose 17% in trials until injection timing was adjusted, and fuel transferred below 50°C clogged filters ([Renewable Energy, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960148123014581)). Specification, heating and blending should be agreed before shipment.

**Bottom line:** keep UCO for SAF and HVO, where it earns the highest premium. Bio heavy oil turns the residues that would otherwise be hard to sell into a fuel with proven buyers in power, industry and shipping. See our [Bio Heavy Oil product page](/products/bio-heavy-oil-malaysia).

## What smart buyers are doing now

The winners won't be the buyers who pay the most for UCO. They'll be the ones with three or four reliable feedstocks they can trace.

1. **Spread volume across several feedstocks.** Use UCO as one stream among several, and add PFAD, SBEO, POME oil or tallow where your plant's pretreatment can handle high free fatty acid content.
2. **Make traceability part of the price.** Regulators are looking more closely at waste claims. POME oil credits are under review, and imported UCO is checked for mislabelled virgin oil ([T&E](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/european-and-us-used-cooking-oil-demand-increasingly-unsustainable-analysis)). ISCC certification, mass-balance records and origin documents now count toward value, not just paperwork ([ISCC](https://www.iscc-system.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ISCC_WnR_Guidance_Document_v1.2_final.pdf)). Our guide to [ISCC certification](/blog/iscc-certification-explained/) explains what to ask for.
3. **Buy close to the source.** Indonesia and Malaysia supply about 85% of the world's palm oil ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/121025-commodities-2026-regulatory-uncertainty-impedes-palm-oil-mill-effluent-trade)), so palm residues such as PFAD, SBEO and POME oil come mainly from Southeast Asia. With Indonesia's exports restricted, Malaysia is the most open origin in the region.
4. **Check eligibility for each destination.** One feedstock can be advanced in one market, single-counted in a second and excluded from SAF in a third. Agree the target market before you agree the price.
5. **Watch the new crops.** Pongamia and carinata are not volume plays yet, but offtake partnerships formed now will decide who has access in 2030.
